Topic 4.1 · Selecting statistical methods

Sampling distributions of sample means

Must know

The sample mean $\bar x$ estimates the population mean $\mu$. For a random sample of size $n$, the sampling distribution of $\bar x$ is centered at $\mu$ with standard deviation $\sigma/\sqrt n$; when $\sigma$ is unknown we estimate that spread with the standard error $s/\sqrt n$. Averages vary less than single observations: multiplying the sample size by four halves the standard deviation.

Every interval and every test in this unit rests on the distribution of $\bar x$, so describe it before computing anything: center, spread and shape. The center answers whether $\bar x$ is fair (it is unbiased for $\mu$). The spread answers how far a typical sample mean sits from the truth ($\sigma/\sqrt n$, estimated by $s/\sqrt n$). The shape answers whether the normal and $t$ models may be used (when the population is approximately normal, or when $n$ is large enough for the central limit theorem).

Sample size $n$ (model: $\mu=25$, $\sigma=4$)SD of $\bar x$Spread against one observation
$n=16$$4/\sqrt{16}=1.0$one quarter of $\sigma$
$n=64$$4/\sqrt{64}=0.5$one eighth of $\sigma$
$n=100$$4/\sqrt{100}=0.4$one tenth of $\sigma$

Synthetic teaching model for this section: a population of commuter wait times with $\mu=25$ minutes and $\sigma=4$ minutes. No actual transit system is described.

Example 4.1 — Describing the sampling distribution of a mean
  1. Setting: waits on one bus route have mean 7.5 minutes and standard deviation 3 minutes, and a commuter records 36 waits chosen at random from more than 500 route runs.
  2. Center: the sampling distribution of $\bar x$ is centered at $\mu=7.5$ minutes, so such samples neither systematically overstate nor understate the route mean.
  3. Spread: $SD(\bar x)=3/\sqrt{36}=0.5$ minutes, so a typical sample mean is about half a minute away from the parameter.
  4. Shape: although individual waits are right-skewed, the skew is not extreme, so at $n=36$ the central limit theorem makes the distribution of $\bar x$ approximately normal.
Go deeper: what a larger sample does and does not fix

A larger sample shrinks the standard deviation of $\bar x$ like $1/\sqrt n$ and makes the normal model safer, but it cannot repair a biased sampling method: the center stays wherever the method aims. A voluntary poll with 10,000 responses still estimates the wrong quantity. A small sample has a wider sampling distribution, not a shifted one.

主题 4.1 · 选择统计方法

样本均值的抽样分布

必备知识

样本均值 $\bar x$ 用来估计总体均值 $\mu$。对于容量为 $n$ 的随机样本，$\bar x$ 的抽样分布以 $\mu$ 为中心，标准差为 $\sigma/\sqrt n$；当 $\sigma$ 未知时，用标准误 $s/\sqrt n$ 估计这一离散程度。均值比单个观测更稳定：样本量变为 4 倍，标准差缩小一半。

本单元的每个区间与检验都建立在 $\bar x$ 的分布之上，因此计算之前先描述它：中心、离散程度与形状。中心回答 $\bar x$ 是否公平（它是 $\mu$ 的无偏估计量）。离散程度回答典型样本均值与真值相差多远（$\sigma/\sqrt n$，用 $s/\sqrt n$ 估计）。形状回答能否使用正态与 $t$ 模型（总体近似正态，或 $n$ 足够大使得中心极限定理成立时即可）。

样本量 $n$（模型：$\mu=25$，$\sigma=4$）$\bar x$ 的标准差与单个观测相比
$n=16$$4/\sqrt{16}=1.0$为 $\sigma$ 的四分之一
$n=64$$4/\sqrt{64}=0.5$为 $\sigma$ 的八分之一
$n=100$$4/\sqrt{100}=0.4$为 $\sigma$ 的十分之一

本节的合成教学模型：某通勤等待时间总体，$\mu=25$ 分钟、$\sigma=4$ 分钟；不描述任何真实的公交系统。

例 4.1 — 描述均值的抽样分布
  1. 情境：某公交线路的等待时间均值为 7.5 分钟、标准差为 3 分钟；一位通勤者随机记录 36 次等待。
  2. 中心：$\bar x$ 的抽样分布以 $\mu=7.5$ 分钟为中心，因此这类样本不会系统性地高估或低估该线路的均值。
  3. 离散程度：$SD(\bar x)=3/\sqrt{36}=0.5$ 分钟，即典型样本均值与参数相差约半分钟。
  4. 形状：当 $n=36$ 时，即使单次等待右偏，中心极限定理也使 $\bar x$ 的分布近似正态。
深入理解：增大样本能解决什么、不能解决什么

增大样本会按 $1/\sqrt n$ 缩小 $\bar x$ 的标准差，并使正态模型更可靠，但它无法修复有偏的抽样方法：中心仍停留在该方法所指向的位置。一个收到 10,000 份回复的自愿网络调查仍然估计着错误的量。样本量小只会让抽样分布更宽，而不会使它偏移。

Topics 4.2 and 4.3 · Analyzing data and interpreting results

One-sample and paired $t$ confidence intervals

Must know

A one-sample $t$-interval for a population mean is $\bar x\pm t^{*}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}$, where $t^{*}$ is the critical value from a $t$ distribution with $n-1$ degrees of freedom. It requires a random sample, the 10% condition, and a sample that is approximately normal (or large enough that the central limit theorem applies). For paired data, subtract within each pair and treat the differences as the one sample; the parameter is the mean difference $\mu_d$.

The $t$ model pays for estimating $\sigma$ with $s$ by using heavier tails than the normal model, so $t^{*}$ is larger than $z^{*}$ for the same confidence level. The gap closes as $n$ grows: with 9 degrees of freedom $t^{*}=2.262$ where $z^{*}=1.960$, and with 29 degrees of freedom $t^{*}=2.045$.

Example 4.2 — A 95% $t$-interval for a mean
  1. Setting: a lab tests 20 random battery cells and finds a mean life of 8.4 hours with $s=1.1$ hours.
  2. Conditions: the cells are a random sample; 20 is at most 10% of a production run; the lifetimes look roughly symmetric with no outliers, so the $t$ model is safe at this sample size.
  3. Standard error: $s/\sqrt n=1.1/\sqrt{20}\approx0.2460$ hours.
  4. Critical value: $t^{*}=2.093$ with $df=20-1=19$.
  5. Margin of error: $2.093(0.2460)\approx0.5148$ hours.
  6. Interval: $8.4\pm0.5148$, which is about $(7.885,\ 8.915)$ hours.
Example 4.3 — Justifying a claim from the interval
  1. Claim to test: the manufacturer advertises a mean life of at least 9 hours.
  2. The 95% interval $(7.885,\ 8.915)$ lies entirely below 9 hours, so no plausible value of $\mu$ in the interval reaches the advertised value.
  3. Wording: at the 95% confidence level the data give evidence against the claim that the mean life reaches 9 hours, because every value the interval allows falls short of it.

A claim is supported only when the whole interval is on the claim's side of the threshold; a claim is contradicted only when the whole interval is on the other side. If the interval straddles the value, the data are inconclusive and the honest sentence says so. Confidence levels are not probabilities about one interval: in repeated random sampling about 95% of intervals built this way capture $\mu$.

Go deeper: paired data change the standard error, not the formula

A random sample of nine runners from a large club each tries the two shoe models in random order, and a dotplot of the differences (new minus old) shows no strong skew or outliers; the differences have mean 12.0 minutes and standard deviation 6.0 minutes. The paired interval uses only the differences: $s_d/\sqrt n=6.0/\sqrt 9=2.0$ and $t^{*}=2.306$ with $df=8$, so the margin is 4.612 and the interval is about $(7.388,\ 16.612)$ minutes of extra endurance. Treating the two columns as independent samples would ignore that each difference is tied to one runner and would use the wrong standard error.

主题 4.2 与 4.3 · 分析数据与解释结果

单样本与配对 $t$ 置信区间

必备知识

总体均值的单样本 $t$ 区间为 $\bar x\pm t^{*}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}$，其中 $t^{*}$ 是自由度为 $n-1$ 的 $t$ 分布的临界值。条件为：随机样本、10% 条件，以及样本近似正态（或样本量足够大使中心极限定理成立）。配对数据则先在同一对内相减，把差值当作单一样本处理，参数为均值差 $\mu_d$。

$t$ 模型以比正态模型更厚的尾部，补偿用 $s$ 估计 $\sigma$ 带来的不确定性，因此同一置信水平下 $t^{*}$ 大于 $z^{*}$。样本量增大后差距缩小：自由度为 9 时 $t^{*}=2.262$，而 $z^{*}=1.960$；自由度为 29 时 $t^{*}=2.045$。

例 4.2 — 均值的 95% $t$ 区间
  1. 情境：实验室随机检测 20 块电池，平均寿命 8.4 小时，$s=1.1$ 小时。
  2. 条件：电池为随机样本；20 不超过该批产量的 10%；寿命分布大致对称且无离群值，故该样本量下 $t$ 模型可用。
  3. 标准误：$s/\sqrt n=1.1/\sqrt{20}\approx0.2460$ 小时。
  4. 临界值：$df=20-1=19$ 时 $t^{*}=2.093$。
  5. 边际误差：$2.093(0.2460)\approx0.5148$ 小时。
  6. 区间：$8.4\pm0.5148$，约 $(7.885,\ 8.915)$ 小时。
例 4.3 — 用区间论证主张
  1. 待检验主张：厂商宣传平均寿命至少 9 小时。
  2. 95% 区间 $(7.885,\ 8.915)$ 完全位于 9 小时之下，因此区间内没有任何 $\mu$ 的合理取值能达到宣传值。
  3. 表述：在 95% 置信水平下，数据提供了反对平均寿命达到 9 小时这一主张的证据，因为区间允许的每个取值都低于它。

只有当整个区间都落在主张阈值的一侧时，该主张才得到支持；只有当整个区间落在另一侧时，该主张才被否定。若区间跨过该阈值，数据不足以判定，诚实的结果就应这样表述。置信水平不是关于某一个区间的概率：在重复随机抽样中，约 95% 的此类区间会覆盖 $\mu$。

深入理解：配对数据改变的是标准误，不是公式

九名跑者各试穿两双鞋，差值（新款减旧款）的均值为 12.0 分钟、标准差为 6.0 分钟。配对区间只使用差值：$s_d/\sqrt n=6.0/\sqrt 9=2.0$，$df=8$ 时 $t^{*}=2.306$，故边际为 4.612，区间约为 $(7.388,\ 16.612)$ 分钟的额外耐力。若把两列当作独立样本，就忽略了每个差值都绑定同一名跑者，从而使用了错误的标准误。

Topics 4.4 and 4.5 · Setting up and carrying out a test

One-sample and paired $t$ tests for a mean

Must know

A one-sample $t$ test compares $H_0:\mu=\mu_0$ with one of $H_a:\mu\gt\mu_0$, $\mu\lt\mu_0$ or $\mu\neq\mu_0$. The test statistic is $t=\frac{\bar x-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt n}$ with $n-1$ degrees of freedom, and the p-value is the probability, computed under the null model, of a result at least as extreme as the one observed in the direction of $H_a$. The same three conditions as the interval are required: random sample, 10% condition, and a roughly normal population or a large sample. Paired data are handled by testing the mean of the differences.

A test answers a yes-or-no question about a claimed value with a stated tolerance for error, alpha. The conclusion never says the null hypothesis is true: failing to reject it only means the data are compatible with it. When the alternative is two-sided, the p-value counts both tails, so a two-sided p-value is twice the one-sided p-value for a symmetric $t$ distribution.

p-value against $\alpha=0.05$DecisionSentence to write
$p\lt 0.05$Reject $H_0$The data give convincing evidence for $H_a$ in context.
$p\gt 0.05$Fail to reject $H_0$The data do not give convincing evidence for $H_a$.
$p=0.05$Reject at $\alpha=0.05$State the convention you used; report the p-value itself.

The tolerance alpha is chosen before the data are examined; changing it after seeing the p-value turns a decision rule into a rationalisation.

Example 4.4 — Setting up a one-sample $t$ test
  1. Setting: a study-skills program claims that its students study more than 2.5 hours per night on average. A random sample of 36 students reports a mean of 2.72 hours with $s=0.6$ hours.
  2. Parameter: $\mu$ is the true mean nightly study time of all students in the program, in hours.
  3. Hypotheses: $H_0:\mu=2.5$ hours and $H_a:\mu\gt 2.5$ hours; the claim lives in the alternative because it asserts more than the benchmark.
  4. Conditions: random sample stated; 36 students is at most 10% of the program; with $n=36$ the central limit theorem covers the shape, and the observed times show no extreme outliers.
  5. Significance level: $\alpha=0.05$, chosen before computing the statistic.
Example 4.5 — Carrying out the test
  1. Standard error: $s/\sqrt n=0.6/\sqrt{36}=0.1$ hour.
  2. Test statistic: $t=(2.72-2.5)/0.1=2.2$ with $df=36-1=35$.
  3. p-value: $P(T\gt 2.2)$ with 35 degrees of freedom is about 0.0173, the upper-tail area because $H_a$ is upper-tailed.
  4. Decision: $0.0173\lt 0.05$, so reject $H_0$.
  5. Conclusion: at the 5% significance level the data give convincing evidence that the mean nightly study time of students in the program exceeds 2.5 hours.
  6. Cross-check: the one-sided critical value is $t^{*}=1.6896$, and $2.2\gt 1.6896$ agrees with the p-value decision. With a two-sided alternative the p-value would double to about 0.0345.
Go deeper: what the p-value is not

The p-value is a probability about the data assuming $H_0$, never a probability that $H_0$ is true, and $1-p$ is not the probability that $H_a$ is true. A small p-value is not a large effect either: with a huge sample a trivial difference can produce a tiny p-value, so report the estimate and its interval beside the test. A non-significant result is also not proof of no effect; it may simply mean the sample was small.

主题 4.4 与 4.5 · 建立假设与执行检验

均值的单样本与配对 $t$ 检验

必备知识

单样本 $t$ 检验把 $H_0:\mu=\mu_0$ 与 $H_a:\mu\gt\mu_0$、$\mu\lt\mu_0$ 或 $\mu\neq\mu_0$ 之一比较。检验统计量为 $t=\frac{\bar x-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt n}$，自由度为 $n-1$；p 值是在原假设模型下，得到与观测结果同样极端或更极端（方向与 $H_a$ 一致）的结果的概率。所需条件与区间相同：随机样本、10% 条件，以及总体近似正态或样本量足够大。配对数据则对差值的均值做检验。

检验以事先设定的容许误差 alpha，回答关于某个声称取值的“是/否”问题。结论从不说原假设为真：不拒绝只意味着数据与它相容。当备择假设为双侧时，p 值要计及两侧尾部，因此在对称的 $t$ 分布下，双侧 p 值等于单侧 p 值的两倍。

p 值与 $\alpha=0.05$ 比较决策应写出的句子
$p\lt 0.05$拒绝 $H_0$数据为 $H_a$ 提供了结合情境的有力证据。
$p\gt 0.05$不拒绝 $H_0$数据未提供支持 $H_a$ 的有力证据。
$p=0.05$在 $\alpha=0.05$ 下拒绝说明所用约定，并直接报告 p 值本身。

容许误差 alpha 在查看数据之前确定；看到 p 值之后再改动它，就把决策规则变成了事后辩解。

例 4.4 — 建立单样本 $t$ 检验
  1. 情境：某学习方法课程声称其学员平均每晚学习超过 2.5 小时。36 名学员的随机样本报告均值为 2.72 小时，$s=0.6$ 小时。
  2. 参数：$\mu$ 为该课程全体学员每晚学习时间的真实均值，单位为小时。
  3. 假设：$H_0:\mu=2.5$ 小时，$H_a:\mu\gt 2.5$ 小时；主张断言“超过”基准，因此位于备择假设。
  4. 条件：已说明随机抽样；36 名学员不超过该课程人数的 10%；$n=36$ 时中心极限定理可解决形状问题，且观测值中无极端离群值。
  5. 显著性水平：在计算统计量之前选定 $\alpha=0.05$。
例 4.5 — 执行检验
  1. 标准误：$s/\sqrt n=0.6/\sqrt{36}=0.1$ 小时。
  2. 检验统计量：$t=(2.72-2.5)/0.1=2.2$，$df=36-1=35$。
  3. p 值：自由度为 35 时 $P(T\gt 2.2)\approx0.0173$，因 $H_a$ 为右尾，故取上尾面积。
  4. 决策：$0.0173\lt 0.05$，拒绝 $H_0$。
  5. 结论：在 5% 显著性水平下，数据提供了有力证据表明该课程学员每晚学习时间的均值超过 2.5 小时。
  6. 交叉验证：单侧临界值 $t^{*}=1.6896$，而 $2.2\gt 1.6896$，与 p 值的决策一致。若备择假设为双侧，p 值将加倍至约 0.0345。
深入理解：p 值不是什么

p 值是在 $H_0$ 成立的前提下关于数据的概率，绝不是 $H_0$ 为真的概率，$1-p$ 也不是 $H_a$ 为真的概率。p 值小也不代表效应大：样本极大时，微不足道的差异也能产生极小的 p 值，因此报告检验时还应给出估计值及其区间。不显著的结果同样不是“没有效应”的证明，也可能只是样本太小。

Topic 4.6 · Selecting statistical methods

Sampling distributions for the difference of two means

Must know

For two independent random samples, the sampling distribution of $\bar x_1-\bar x_2$ is centered at $\mu_1-\mu_2$ with standard deviation $\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{\sigma_2^{2}}{n_2}}$, estimated by $SE=\sqrt{\frac{s_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^{2}}{n_2}}$. The variances add; the standard deviations never do. The distribution is approximately normal when both populations are approximately normal, or when both samples are large.

Comparing two means means comparing them by subtracting. The center of the difference is the difference of the centers, and the spread grows because each sample brings its own uncertainty: two noisy estimates produce a noisier difference. This is also why increasing one sample size only shrinks the term it owns.

Example 4.6 — Describing a difference of means
  1. Setting: an engineer compares a new curing process with the current one, sampling 25 and 36 units and recording strength in megapascals.
  2. Center: the sampling distribution of $\bar x_{new}-\bar x_{old}$ is centered at the true mean difference, so the sample difference is unbiased for it.
  3. Spread: with the model's values, $SD(\bar x_{new}-\bar x_{old})=1.118$ MPa, larger than either single-sample standard deviation of 1.0 and 0.5 MPa, because both samples contribute variability.
  4. Shape: if both population distributions are roughly symmetric with no strong outliers, then with $n_1=25$ and $n_2=36$, both sample means are approximately normal, so their difference is too.
  5. Independence: the two samples must be chosen independently of each other; the 10% condition applies to each population separately.
Go deeper: why the variances add

Subtracting an independent random quantity adds its uncertainty instead of cancelling it: $\bar x_2$ moves up and down in its own right, so the difference inherits both wobbles. Adding variances is exactly what the independence assumption buys. If the two samples were paired (the same units measured twice), the difference of means would be the mean of the pairwise differences, whose standard deviation can be much smaller, and the two-sample formula above would be wrong.

主题 4.6 · 选择统计方法

两个均值之差的抽样分布

必备知识

对于两个独立的随机样本，$\bar x_1-\bar x_2$ 的抽样分布以 $\mu_1-\mu_2$ 为中心，标准差为 $\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{\sigma_2^{2}}{n_2}}$，并用 $SE=\sqrt{\frac{s_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^{2}}{n_2}}$ 估计。方差相加，标准差绝不直接相加。当两个总体都近似正态，或两个样本都足够大时，该分布近似正态。

比较两个均值就是做减法。差值的中心是中心之差，而离散程度会变大，因为每个样本都带来自身的不确定性：两个带有噪声的估计相减，得到的差值噪声更大。这也解释了为什么增大其中一个样本量只能缩小属于它的那一项。

量下侧模型中的取值
总体 1：$n_1=25$，$\sigma_1=5$$\sigma_1^{2}/n_1=25/25=1.00$
总体 2：$n_2=36$，$\sigma_2=3$$\sigma_2^{2}/n_2=9/36=0.25$
$\bar x_1-\bar x_2$ 的中心$\mu_1-\mu_2$
$\bar x_1-\bar x_2$ 的标准差$\sqrt{1.00+0.25}=1.118$

合成教学模型：两个总体的标准差与样本量如上；不涉及任何真实测量。

例 4.6 — 描述均值之差
  1. 情境：某工程师比较新的固化工艺与现有工艺，分别抽取 25 件与 36 件样品，记录抗压强度（兆帕）。
  2. 中心：$\bar x_{new}-\bar x_{old}$ 的抽样分布以真实均值差为中心，因此样本差值是该参数的无偏估计。
  3. 离散程度：按模型取值，$SD(\bar x_{new}-\bar x_{old})=1.118$ 兆帕，大于两个单样本标准差 1.0 与 0.5 兆帕，因为两个样本都贡献了变异。
  4. 形状：当 $n_1=25$、$n_2=36$ 时，两个样本均值都近似正态，故其差值也近似正态。
  5. 独立性：两个样本必须彼此独立抽取；10% 条件需分别对每个总体核验。
深入理解：为什么方差相加

减去一个独立的随机量时，它的不确定性是叠加而非抵消：$\bar x_2$ 自身也在上下波动，差值便同时继承了两者的波动。方差相加正是独立性假设所带来的结果。若两个样本是配对的（同一批对象测量两次），均值之差就等于各对差值的均值，其标准差可能小得多，上面的两样本公式在此并不适用。

Topics 4.7 and 4.8 · Constructing an interval and justifying a claim

Two-sample $t$ confidence intervals

Must know

A two-sample $t$-interval for $\mu_1-\mu_2$ is $(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)\pm t^{*}SE$ with $SE=\sqrt{\frac{s_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^{2}}{n_2}}$. The degrees of freedom are $\min(n_1,n_2)-1$ by the conservative rule used in this course, or the larger value reported by technology. Conditions: two independent random samples, the 10% condition for each, and both populations approximately normal or both samples large. Do not pool the standard deviations and do not use the paired formula.

The interval estimates the size of a difference, so its interpretation names both populations and the direction of the subtraction. Order matters for the sign: computing $\bar x_A-\bar x_B$ makes a positive interval mean group A is larger on average.

Interval versus 0Conclusion about $\mu_1-\mu_2$Claim supported
Entirely above 0Plausible values are all positive$\mu_1\gt\mu_2$
Entirely below 0Plausible values are all negative$\mu_1\lt\mu_2$
Contains 0Zero remains plausibleNo evidence of a difference
Example 4.7 — Constructing the two-sample interval
  1. Setting: two teaching methods are compared on the same 40-point unit test. At a school with 800 students, 45 students were randomly assigned to the two methods. Method A: $n_1=20$, $\bar x_1=30.4$ points, $s_1=8$ points. Method B: $n_2=25$, $\bar x_2=25.4$ points, $s_2=6$ points.
  2. Conditions: the two treatment groups are independent because students were randomly assigned, which supports comparing the two methods; with 20 and 25 students the $t$ model is acceptable and no strong skew is reported.
  3. Standard error: $SE=\sqrt{8^{2}/20+6^{2}/25}=\sqrt{3.2+1.44}\approx2.1541$ points.
  4. Degrees of freedom and critical value: $\min(20,25)-1=19$, so $t^{*}=2.093$ for 95% confidence.
  5. Margin of error: $2.093(2.1541)\approx4.5085$ points.
  6. Interval: $5.0\pm4.5085$, about $(0.491,\ 9.509)$ points, where 5.0 is $\bar x_A-\bar x_B$.
Example 4.8 — Justifying a claim about the difference
  1. Claim to examine: method A raises the mean score by at least 3 points, that is $\mu_A-\mu_B\ge 3$.
  2. The 95% interval $(0.491,\ 9.509)$ contains 3, so the data do not establish an advantage of 3 points or more.
  3. What is supported: because the interval lies entirely above 0, the data do provide evidence that the mean score under method A is greater than under method B, by somewhere between about 0.5 and 9.5 points.
  4. Scope: the conclusion is about these two teaching methods as administered, not about the methods in every setting, and it is not an explanation of why A performed better.
Go deeper: degrees of freedom and what they cost

The conservative rule $\min(n_1,n_2)-1$ uses the smaller sample to stay on the safe side, which makes $t^{*}$ larger and the interval wider. Technology reports a larger approximate value computed from both sample variances, giving a slightly narrower interval; both are accepted on the AP exam, but the choice must be stated and used consistently within one answer. Equal population standard deviations are not required by this procedure, so there is no pooling step.

主题 4.7 与 4.8 · 构造区间与论证主张

两样本 $t$ 置信区间

必备知识

$\mu_1-\mu_2$ 的两样本 $t$ 区间为 $(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)\pm t^{*}SE$，其中 $SE=\sqrt{\frac{s_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^{2}}{n_2}}$。自由度按本课程使用的保守规则取 $\min(n_1,n_2)-1$，或采用技术工具给出的较大取值。条件：两个独立的随机样本、各自满足 10% 条件，以及两个总体都近似正态或两个样本都足够大。不要合并标准差，也不要使用配对公式。

该区间估计的是差值的大小，因此解释时必须点明两个总体以及相减的方向。顺序决定符号：计算 $\bar x_A-\bar x_B$ 时，区间为正说明 A 组平均更高。

区间与 0 的关系关于 $\mu_1-\mu_2$ 的结论所支持的主张
完全在 0 之上所有合理取值均为正$\mu_1\gt\mu_2$
完全在 0 之下所有合理取值均为负$\mu_1\lt\mu_2$
包含 00 仍是合理取值没有证据表明存在差异
例 4.7 — 构造两样本区间
  1. 情境：在同一份 40 分的单元测验上比较两种教学法。在一所有 800 名学生的学校中，45 名学生被随机分配到两种教学法。方法 A：$n_1=20$，$\bar x_1=30.4$ 分，$s_1=8$ 分；方法 B：$n_2=25$，$\bar x_2=25.4$ 分，$s_2=6$ 分。
  2. 条件：学生被随机分配，因此两个处理组相互独立，可以比较两种教学法；样本量为 20 与 25 时 $t$ 模型可接受，且未报告明显偏态。
  3. 标准误：$SE=\sqrt{8^{2}/20+6^{2}/25}=\sqrt{3.2+1.44}\approx2.1541$ 分。
  4. 自由度与临界值：$\min(20,25)-1=19$，95% 置信下 $t^{*}=2.093$。
  5. 边际误差：$2.093(2.1541)\approx4.5085$ 分。
  6. 区间：$5.0\pm4.5085$，约 $(0.491,\ 9.509)$ 分，其中 5.0 为 $\bar x_A-\bar x_B$。
例 4.8 — 论证关于差值的主张
  1. 待考察主张：方法 A 使平均分至少提高 3 分，即 $\mu_A-\mu_B\ge 3$。
  2. 95% 区间 $(0.491,\ 9.509)$ 包含 3，故数据无法确立 3 分或更多的优势。
  3. 可以得到支持的部分：由于区间完全在 0 之上，数据确实提供了方法 A 的平均分高于方法 B 的证据，差值大致在 0.5 分与 9.5 分之间。
  4. 适用范围：结论针对按上述方式实施的这两种教学法，而不是它们在所有情形下的表现，也没有解释 A 表现更好的原因。
深入理解：自由度的取舍

保守规则 $\min(n_1,n_2)-1$ 取较小的样本量以守住安全一侧，使 $t^{*}$ 更大、区间更宽。技术工具会依据两个样本方差给出更大的近似值，区间略窄；两者在 AP 考试中都可接受，但必须在同一份解答中说明选择并保持一致。该程序不要求两个总体标准差相等，因此没有合并步骤。

Topics 4.9 and 4.10 · Setting up and carrying out a test for a difference

Two-sample $t$ tests

Must know

A two-sample $t$ test compares $H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0$ with an alternative that is upper-tailed, lower-tailed or two-sided. The statistic is $t=\frac{\bar x_1-\bar x_2}{SE}$ with $SE=\sqrt{\frac{s_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^{2}}{n_2}}$ and the same degrees of freedom as the interval. The conditions are those of topic 4.6: two independent random samples, the 10% condition for each, and approximately normal populations or large samples.

The two-sample test and the two-sample interval answer the same question in two currencies: at a two-sided $\alpha=0.05$, the test rejects exactly when the 95% interval excludes 0. Reporting both is therefore a consistency check, not extra work.

Hypotheses$H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0$ against the alternative the claim asserts
ConditionsIndependent random samples, 10% each, shape satisfied by the sample sizes or by the populations
Statistic$t=(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)/SE$ with the degrees of freedom stated
p-value and decisionArea in the direction of $H_a$, compared with $\alpha$, followed by a conclusion in context
Example 4.9 — Setting up the test
  1. Question: do the two teaching methods differ in mean test score?
  2. Parameter: $\mu_A-\mu_B$, the difference in true mean scores, in points, between the two methods.
  3. Hypotheses: $H_0:\mu_A-\mu_B=0$ and $H_a:\mu_A-\mu_B\neq 0$; the question asks whether they differ, without a direction, so the alternative is two-sided.
  4. Conditions: both classes were assigned at random and are independent of each other; each is at most 10% of its population; 20 and 25 students make the $t$ model usable.
  5. Significance level: $\alpha=0.05$.
Example 4.10 — Carrying out the test
  1. Standard error: $SE=\sqrt{8^{2}/20+6^{2}/25}pprox2.1541$ points, the same value the interval used.
  2. Test statistic: $t=(30.4-25.4)/2.1541pprox2.3212$ with $df=19$ under the conservative rule.
  3. p-value: the two-sided area with 19 degrees of freedom is about 0.0315.
  4. Decision: $0.0315\lt 0.05$, so reject $H_0$.
  5. Conclusion: at the 5% significance level the data provide convincing evidence that the two methods differ in mean unit-test score.
  6. Consistency: the 95% interval $(0.491,\ 9.509)$ excludes 0, exactly as the two-sided test at $\alpha=0.05$ requires. A one-sided alternative would halve the p-value to about 0.0158, but the direction would have to be justified before looking at the data.
Go deeper: rejecting a difference is not explaining it

A significant test says the observed difference is hard to attribute to sampling variability alone; it does not say the difference is large, nor why it exists. A randomised experiment supports a causal conclusion about the treatment, while an observational comparison supports only an association, because confounding variables remain in the picture. Report the size of the difference with its interval so a reader can judge whether the effect matters.

主题 4.9 与 4.10 · 建立并执行差值检验

两样本 $t$ 检验

必备知识

两样本 $t$ 检验把 $H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0$ 与上尾、下尾或双侧备择假设比较。统计量为 $t=\frac{\bar x_1-\bar x_2}{SE}$，其中 $SE=\sqrt{\frac{s_1^{2}}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^{2}}{n_2}}$，自由度与区间相同。条件即主题 4.6 的条件：两个独立的随机样本、各自满足 10% 条件，以及总体近似正态或样本量足够大。

两样本检验与两样本区间用两种方式回答同一个问题：在双侧 $\alpha=0.05$ 下，检验拒绝 $H_0$ 当且仅当 95% 区间不包含 0。因此同时报告两者是一致性核验，而不是额外工作。

假设$H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0$，与主张所断言方向的备择假设
条件独立的随机样本、各自 10% 条件、由样本量或总体保证形状
统计量$t=(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)/SE$，并说明自由度

例 4.9 — 建立检验
  1. 问题：两种教学法的平均测验分数是否存在差异？
  2. 参数：$\mu_A-\mu_B$，即两种教学法真实平均分之差，单位为分。
  3. 假设：$H_0:\mu_A-\mu_B=0$，$H_a:\mu_A-\mu_B\neq 0$；问题只问是否有差异、未指定方向，故备择假设为双侧。
  4. 条件：两个班均被随机分配，且彼此独立；各自不超过其总体的 10%；20 与 25 名学生使 $t$ 模型可用。
  5. 显著性水平：$\alpha=0.05$。
例 4.10 — 执行检验
  1. 标准误：$SE=\sqrt{8^{2}/20+6^{2}/25}pprox2.1541$ 分，与区间所用数值相同。
  2. 检验统计量：$t=(30.4-25.4)/2.1541pprox2.3212$，按保守规则 $df=19$。
  3. p 值：自由度为 19 的双侧面积约为 0.0315。
  4. 决策：$0.0315\lt 0.05$，拒绝 $H_0$。
  5. 结论：在 5% 显著性水平下，数据提供了有力证据表明两种教学法的单元测验平均分存在差异。
  6. 一致性：95% 区间 $(0.491,\ 9.509)$ 不包含 0，与双侧检验在 $\alpha=0.05$ 下的要求完全一致。若为单侧备择假设，p 值将减半至约 0.0158，但其方向必须在查看数据之前说明理由。
深入理解：拒绝差异不等于解释差异

显著的检验结果说明观测到的差异难以仅用抽样波动解释，但它并不意味着差异大，也不解释差异的成因。随机实验支持关于处理因素的因果结论，观察性比较只支持相关关系，因为混杂变量仍在其中。报告差异大小时应附上其区间，读者才能判断该效应是否重要。

Exam writing toolkit

Choosing a procedure

One sample of measurements: one-sample $t$ interval or test. Two measurements on the same units (before and after, matched pairs): subtract within pairs and use the one-sample $t$ procedure on the differences, with $df=n-1$ where $n$ is the number of pairs. Two independent groups: two-sample $t$ interval or test, with $SE=\sqrt{s_1^{2}/n_1+s_2^{2}/n_2}$ and $df=\min(n_1,n_2)-1$ unless technology is stated.

Model response 1 (one-sample $t$ interval)

Question. A lab tests 20 randomly selected battery cells and finds a mean life of 8.4 hours with a standard deviation of 1.1 hours. (a) State and check the conditions for a one-sample $t$ interval. (b) Compute the standard error, the margin of error and the 95% interval. (c) Interpret the interval. (d) The manufacturer advertises a mean life of at least 9 hours. What does the interval say about that claim?

Model. (a) The cells are a random sample, and 20 is at most 10% of a production run, so the observations are independent enough. The lifetimes show no strong skew or outliers, so the population is plausibly normal and the $t$ model is safe at $n=20$. (b) $SE=1.1/\sqrt{20}=0.2460$ hours; with $df=19$, $t^{*}=2.093$; $ME=2.093(0.2460)=0.5148$ hours; the interval is $8.4\pm0.5148$, about $(7.885,\ 8.915)$ hours. (c) We are 95% confident that the true mean life of this population of cells is between about 7.885 and 8.915 hours. (d) Every value in the interval lies below 9 hours, so the interval contradicts the advertised mean rather than supporting it.

Model response 2 (two-sample $t$ test)

Question. Two teaching methods are compared on a 40-point test. Method A: $n_1=20$, $\bar x_1=30.4$, $s_1=8$. Method B: $n_2=25$, $\bar x_2=25.4$, $s_2=6$. Students were randomly assigned. Is there evidence that the mean scores differ?

Model. Parameter: $\mu_A-\mu_B$, the difference in true mean scores. Hypotheses: $H_0:\mu_A-\mu_B=0$ and $H_a:\mu_A-\mu_B\neq 0$. Conditions: the groups are independent random samples, each under 10% of its population, and with 20 and 25 students the sampling distribution of the difference is approximately normal. Statistics: $SE=\sqrt{8^2/20+6^2/25}=2.1541$; $t=5.0/2.1541=2.321$; with $df=\min(20,25)-1=19$ the two-sided p-value is 0.0315. Decision: $0.0315\lt 0.05$, so reject $H_0$. Conclusion: the data give convincing evidence that the two methods differ in mean unit-test score, by an estimated 5.0 points with a 95% interval of about 0.49 to 9.51 points. Because students were randomly assigned, the difference can be attributed to the method, though the interval is wide enough that the practical size of the advantage is not pinned down.

Go deeper: what earns the marks

Marks attach to the reasons, not the arithmetic: naming the parameter, verifying each condition with the sample's own numbers, showing the substituted standard error and the degrees of freedom, and writing the final sentence in context. A correct number with no condition check loses the condition marks, and an interval reported without the confidence level or the parameter loses the interpretation mark.

Diagnostics

Common errors and their corrections

Error in the draftCorrectionTag
"The sampling distribution is just the data we collected."The data are one sample; the sampling distribution describes how a statistic such as $\bar x$ would vary over all possible samples of this size.M17
"There is a 95% probability that $\mu$ is in this interval."Confidence describes the method: about 95% of intervals built this way capture $\mu$. Once computed, the interval either contains $\mu$ or it does not.M18
"The p-value is the probability that $H_0$ is true."The p-value is computed assuming $H_0$ is true: it is the probability of a result at least as extreme as the observed one.M19
Using $\sigma/\sqrt n$ or a pooled standard deviation in an interval, or $\sigma$ instead of $s$.Intervals and tests for means use $s/\sqrt n$ for one mean, $s_d/\sqrt n$ for paired differences, and $\sqrt{s_1^{2}/n_1+s_2^{2}/n_2}$ for two independent means, with no pooling.M20
"We rejected $H_0$, so $H_0$ must be false" or confusing the two error types.Rejecting a true $H_0$ is a Type I error with probability $\alpha$; failing to reject a false $H_0$ is a Type II error, and the power of a test is $1-\beta$.M21
Treating two measurements of the same units as two independent samples.Matched pairs are analysed as one sample of differences; the two-sample formula would ignore the pairing and use the wrong standard error.M22
Running a $t$ procedure with no condition check, or checking only randomness.State random sampling (or random assignment), the 10% condition, and the normality evidence: a normal population, a large sample, or the absence of strong skew and outliers.M23
Adding standard deviations when combining two samples.Variances add under independence: $SE=\sqrt{s_1^{2}/n_1+s_2^{2}/n_2}$. Adding the standard deviations overstates the spread.M16
"The sample is large, so the bias goes away."Large samples reduce variability, not bias. The random-selection condition is what makes the estimate fair.M6
"The difference was not significant, so the treatment has no effect."A non-significant result means the data are compatible with no effect, not that the effect is zero; a small sample may simply lack power.M12

诊断

常见错误与修正

草稿中的错误修正标签
“抽样分布就是我们收集到的这组数据。”数据只是一个样本；抽样分布描述的是诸如 $\bar x$ 的统计量在容量相同的所有可能样本之间的变化。M17
“$\mu$ 落在这个区间内的概率是 95%。”置信描述的是方法：如此构造的区间约有 95% 会覆盖 $\mu$。区间一旦算出，它是否包含 $\mu$ 就已确定。M18
“p 值是 $H_0$ 为真的概率。”p 值是在假定 $H_0$ 为真的前提下计算的：它刻画观测结果或更极端结果出现的概率。M19
区间中使用 $\sigma/\sqrt n$ 或合并标准差，或把 $\sigma$ 当作 $s$。均值的区间与检验使用：单均值 $s/\sqrt n$；配对差值 $s_d/\sqrt n$；两个独立均值 $\sqrt{s_1^{2}/n_1+s_2^{2}/n_2}$，且不作合并。M20
“我们拒绝了 $H_0$，所以 $H_0$ 必为假”，或混淆两类错误。拒绝为真的 $H_0$ 是第一类错误，概率为 $\alpha$；不拒绝为假的 $H_0$ 是第二类错误，检验的功效为 $1-\beta$。M21
把同一对象的两次测量当作两个独立样本。配对数据按差值的单一样本处理；两样本公式会忽略配对关系并使用错误的标准误。M22
未核验条件就使用 $t$ 程序，或只核验随机性。写出随机抽样（或随机分配）、10% 条件与正态性依据：总体正态、样本量足够大，或没有明显偏态与离群值。M23
合并两个样本时把标准差相加。在独立假设下方差相加：$SE=\sqrt{s_1^{2}/n_1+s_2^{2}/n_2}$。直接相加标准差会夸大离散程度。M16
“样本很大，偏差就消失了。”大样本降低的是变异，不是偏差。使估计无偏的是随机抽样条件。M6
“差异不显著，说明处理没有效果。”不显著意味着数据与“无效应”相容，而不是效应为零；样本过小可能只是功效不足。M12

Unit quiz

1. A population has standard deviation 5.0. For random samples of size 25, what is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample mean?

Answer: A. The standard deviation of $\bar x$ is $\sigma/\sqrt n=5.0/\sqrt{25}=1.0$.

  • A. Correct: $5.0/\sqrt{25}=1.0$.
  • B. This divides $\sigma$ by $n$ instead of by $\sqrt n$.
  • C. This is the population standard deviation, not the spread of the sample mean.
  • D. This is $\sigma^{2}/n^{2}$, the squared spread scaled twice.
2. A 95% $t$-interval is built from a random sample of 10 observations. Which critical value is correct?

Answer: A. With $n=10$ the degrees of freedom are $10-1=9$, and the middle 95% of that $t$ distribution is bounded by $\pm2.262$.

  • A. Correct: $df=9$ gives $t^{*}=2.262$.
  • B. $1.960$ is the normal critical value; it ignores the extra uncertainty from estimating $\sigma$ with $s$.
  • C. $1.833$ leaves only 5% in one tail, but a 95% interval needs 2.5% in each tail.
  • D. $2.228$ would be right for 11 observations, where $df=10$.
3. A one-sample $t$ test with 35 degrees of freedom gives $t=2.2$ and a one-sided p-value of 0.0173. Which interpretation is correct?

Answer: A. The p-value is computed under the assumption that $H_0$ is true and measures how unusual the observed statistic would be.

  • A. Correct: a probability about the data, assuming the null model.
  • B. This reverses the conditioning; the p-value is not a probability about the hypothesis.
  • C. The p-value is a tail area of the $t$ distribution, not a probability attached to a point value.
  • D. The p-value is not a share of the population.
4. A 95% $t$-interval for the mean commute time of a city's commuters is $(24.1,\ 31.8)$ minutes. A report claims the mean commute is 34 minutes. What does the interval say?

Answer: A. A claim is contradicted when the entire interval sits on the other side of the claimed value.

  • A. Correct: every plausible value of $\mu$ is below 34 minutes.
  • B. Closeness to an endpoint is not support: 34 lies outside the interval, so it is not a plausible value of the mean.
  • C. The interval is about the population mean, so it speaks directly to such claims.
  • D. A value outside an interval is not thereby true; the interval simply gives no support for it.
5. Independent samples are drawn with $n_1=25$, $\sigma_1=5$ and $n_2=36$, $\sigma_2=3$. What is the standard deviation of $\bar x_1-\bar x_2$?

Answer: A. The variances add: $\sqrt{25/25+9/36}=\sqrt{1.25}=1.118$.

  • A. Correct: variances add, then take the square root.
  • B. This adds the standard deviations, $5+3$, which overstates the spread.
  • C. This is only the second term of the variance sum.
  • D. This is the variance sum; the standard deviation is its square root.
6. A two-sided two-sample $t$ test uses $\bar x_1-\bar x_2=5.0$ and $SE=2.154$ with $df=19$. What are the statistic and the decision at $\alpha=0.05$?

Answer: A. $t=5.0/2.154=2.321$; with $df=19$ the two-sided area beyond $\pm2.321$ is about 0.0315, which is less than 0.05.

  • A. Correct: divide the difference by its standard error, then compare p with $\alpha$.
  • B. The size of the difference in the units of the problem does not decide a test; the p-value does.
  • C. The statistic divides the estimate by its standard error, not the reverse.
  • D. Multiplying instead of dividing gives a value with the wrong scale entirely.
7. A 95% two-sample $t$-interval for $\mu_A-\mu_B$ is $(0.491,\ 9.509)$ points. Which conclusion is supported?

Answer: A. The interval estimates the difference of means, so a wholly positive interval supports a positive difference.

  • A. Correct: all plausible values of the difference are positive.
  • B. The interval is about the mean difference, not about individual students' scores.
  • C. Containing a value does not pin the parameter to it; the interval lists plausible values.
  • D. The interval cannot assign a probability to a hypothesis.
8. Nine runners each test two shoe models, and the differences in endurance (new minus old) are recorded. Which procedure estimates the mean effect of the new model?

Answer: A. Within-runner pairs are analysed as one sample of differences, which accounts for runner-to-runner variability.

  • A. Correct: the differences are the sample, and there are 9 of them.
  • B. Treating the two models as independent samples ignores the pairing and uses the wrong standard error.
  • C. Pooling all 18 measurements mixes the between-runner variability into the estimate.
  • D. The outcome is a measured number of minutes, not a proportion of successes.

  • C. 区间针对的是总体均值，因此直接回答此类主张。
  • D. 落在区间之外并不意味着为真，区间只是不支持它。

  • B. 这里把标准差相加，$5+3$，夸大了离散程度。
  • C. 这只是方差和的第二项。
  • D. 这是方差和；标准差是它的平方根。
6. 某双侧两样本 $t$ 检验中 $\bar x_1-\bar x_2=5.0$，$SE=2.154$，$df=19$。在 $\alpha=0.05$ 下统计量与决策如何？

答案：A。 $t=5.0/2.154=2.321$；$df=19$ 时 $\pm2.321$ 之外的双侧面积约为 0.0315，小于 0.05。

  • A. 正确：用差值除以其标准误，再把 p 与 $\alpha$ 比较。
  • B. 差异在题目单位下的大小不能决定检验，p 值才能。
  • C. 统计量是估计值除以其标准误，不是反过来。
  • D. 相乘而非相除得到的数值量纲完全不对。

答案：A。 该区间估计的是均值之差，因此区间全为正说明差异为正。

  • A. 正确：差值的所有合理取值均为正。
  • B. 区间针对均值差，而不是单个学生的分数。
  • C. 包含某个取值不等于该参数就等于它；区间给出的是合理取值范围。
  • D. 区间无法为假设赋予概率。
8. 九名跑者各测试两种鞋型，记录耐力差值（新款减旧款）。哪种程序可估计新款鞋的平均效应？

答案：A。 同一位跑者内的随机配对应按差值的单一样本分析，从而消除跑者之间的差异。

  • A. 正确：差值就是样本，共 9 个。
  • B. 把两种鞋型当作独立样本会忽略配对关系，并使用错误的标准误。
  • C. 合并 18 个测量值会把跑者之间的差异混入估计。
  • D. 结果是分钟数，不是成功比例。
