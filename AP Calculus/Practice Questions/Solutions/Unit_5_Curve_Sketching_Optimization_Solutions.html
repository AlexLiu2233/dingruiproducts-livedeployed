FRQ 13HARD 5.12 A Tilted Ellipse, Analysed Implicitly5.12 斜椭圆的隐函数分析No Calculator[10 marks]

The curve $C$ is $x^2+xy+y^2=12$.曲线 $C$ 由 $x^2+xy+y^2=12$ 定义。

Answers:答案：  (a) $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{2x+y}{x+2y}$; tangent at $(2,2)$: $y=-x+4$$(2,2)$ 处切线：$y=-x+4$  ·  (b) horizontal at $(2,-4),(-2,4)$; vertical at $(-4,2),(4,-2)$水平切线于 $(2,-4),(-2,4)$；竖直切线于 $(-4,2),(4,-2)$  ·  (c) $(2,-4)$: $y''=\frac13$, local min; $(-2,4)$: $y''=-\frac13$, local max$(2,-4)$：$y''=\frac13$，极小；$(-2,4)$：$y''=-\frac13$，极大  ·  (d) $-4\le y\le4$

(a) Implicit differentiation and tangent line(a) 隐函数求导与切线 M1·A1·A1

Differentiate $x^2+xy+y^2=12$: $2x+y+xy'+2yy'=0$, so $(x+2y)y'=-(2x+y)$ and $y'=-\dfrac{2x+y}{x+2y}$. (M1·A1)对 $x^2+xy+y^2=12$ 求导：$2x+y+xy'+2yy'=0$，故 $(x+2y)y'=-(2x+y)$，$y'=-\dfrac{2x+y}{x+2y}$。(M1·A1)

At $(2,2)$, $y'=-\dfrac{4+2}{2+4}=-1$, so the tangent is $y-2=-(x-2)$, i.e. $y=-x+4$. (A1)在 $(2,2)$ 处，$y'=-\dfrac{4+2}{2+4}=-1$，故切线为 $y-2=-(x-2)$，即 $y=-x+4$。(A1)

(b) Horizontal and vertical tangents(b) 水平切线与竖直切线 M1·A1·A1·R1

Horizontal: numerator $2x+y=0$, so $y=-2x$. Substitute into $C$: $x^2-2x^2+4x^2=3x^2=12$, so $x=\pm2$. The points are $(2,-4)$ and $(-2,4)$. Their denominators are $-6$ and $6$, nonzero, so the tangents are horizontal. (M1·A1)水平：分子 $2x+y=0$，故 $y=-2x$。代入 $C$：$x^2-2x^2+4x^2=3x^2=12$，故 $x=\pm2$。点为 $(2,-4)$ 与 $(-2,4)$。其分母分别为 $-6$ 与 $6$，均非零，故切线水平。(M1·A1)

Vertical: denominator $x+2y=0$, so $x=-2y$. Substitute: $4y^2-2y^2+y^2=3y^2=12$, so $y=\pm2$. The points are $(-4,2)$ and $(4,-2)$. Their numerators are $-6$ and $6$, nonzero, so the tangents are vertical. (A1·R1)竖直：分母 $x+2y=0$，故 $x=-2y$。代入：$4y^2-2y^2+y^2=3y^2=12$，故 $y=\pm2$。点为 $(-4,2)$ 与 $(4,-2)$。其分子分别为 $-6$ 与 $6$，均非零，故切线竖直。(A1·R1)

(c) Second derivative at horizontal tangents(c) 水平切线处的二阶导数 M1·A1·A1·R1

Write $N=2x+y$, $D=x+2y$, so $y'=-N/D$. Then $y''=-\dfrac{(2+y')D-N(1+2y')}{D^2}$. At a horizontal tangent, $y'=0$ and $N=0$, so $y''=-\dfrac{2D}{D^2}=-\dfrac{2}{D}=-\dfrac{2}{x+2y}$. (M1·A1)记 $N=2x+y$，$D=x+2y$，则 $y'=-N/D$。于是 $y''=-\dfrac{(2+y')D-N(1+2y')}{D^2}$。在水平切线处，$y'=0$ 且 $N=0$，故 $y''=-\dfrac{2D}{D^2}=-\dfrac{2}{D}=-\dfrac{2}{x+2y}$。(M1·A1)

At $(2,-4)$: $x+2y=2-8=-6$, so $y''=-\dfrac{2}{-6}=\dfrac13\gt0$; thus $y$ has a local minimum there. At $(-2,4)$: $x+2y=-2+8=6$, so $y''=-\dfrac{2}{6}=-\dfrac13\lt0$; thus $y$ has a local maximum there. (A1·A1·R1)在 $(2,-4)$ 处：$x+2y=2-8=-6$，故 $y''=-\dfrac{2}{-6}=\dfrac13\gt0$；因此 $y$ 在该处有极小值。在 $(-2,4)$ 处：$x+2y=-2+8=6$，故 $y''=-\dfrac{2}{6}=-\dfrac13\lt0$；因此 $y$ 在该处有极大值。(A1·A1·R1)

(d) A derivative-free bound for $y$(d) 不用导数的 $y$ 范围论证 M1·A1

Treat $x^2+xy+y^2-12=0$ as a quadratic in $x$: $x^2+yx+(y^2-12)=0$. For a real point on $C$, the discriminant must be nonnegative: $y^2-4(y^2-12)=48-3y^2\ge0$. (M1)把 $x^2+xy+y^2-12=0$ 看作关于 $x$ 的二次方程：$x^2+yx+(y^2-12)=0$。对 $C$ 上的实点，判别式须非负：$y^2-4(y^2-12)=48-3y^2\ge0$。(M1)

Thus $y^2\le16$, so $-4\le y\le4$. Equality occurs at $y=-4$ with $x=2$, and at $y=4$ with $x=-2$, the horizontal-tangent points. (A1)故 $y^2\le16$，即 $-4\le y\le4$。等号在 $y=-4,x=2$ 与 $y=4,x=-2$ 处取得，即水平切线点。(A1)

Insight.要点。 A horizontal tangent needs numerator $0$ and denominator nonzero; a vertical tangent needs denominator $0$ and numerator nonzero. The point $(0,0)$ makes both zero but is not on $C$. At horizontal tangents the second-derivative formula simplifies dramatically, and the discriminant argument gives the same $y$-range without calculus.水平切线要求分子为 $0$ 且分母非零；竖直切线要求分母为 $0$ 且分子非零。$(0,0)$ 使两者同时为零，但不在 $C$ 上。水平切线处二阶导数公式大大简化；判别式论证则不用微积分给出同一个 $y$ 范围。

(a) Intercepts and end behaviour(a) 截距与端点行为 A1·A1

$f(x)=0$ gives $x^{2/3}=0$ or $x-5=0$, so the $x$-intercepts are $(0,0)$ and $(5,0)$. (A1)$f(x)=0$ 得 $x^{2/3}=0$ 或 $x-5=0$，故 $x$ 轴截距为 $(0,0)$ 与 $(5,0)$。(A1)

(b) First derivative, critical points and cusp(b) 一阶导数、临界点与尖点 M1·A1·A1·R1

$f(x)=x^{5/3}-5x^{2/3}$, so $f'(x)=\dfrac53x^{2/3}-\dfrac{10}{3}x^{-1/3}=\dfrac{5(x-2)}{3x^{1/3}}$ for $x\ne0$. (M1·A1)$f(x)=x^{5/3}-5x^{2/3}$，故 $f'(x)=\dfrac53x^{2/3}-\dfrac{10}{3}x^{-1/3}=\dfrac{5(x-2)}{3x^{1/3}}$，$x\ne0$。(M1·A1)

$f'=0$ at $x=2$; $f'$ does not exist at $x=0$. Sign chart: $f'\gt0$ for $x\lt0$, $f'\lt0$ for $0\lt x\lt2$, and $f'\gt0$ for $x\gt2$.
